# Adapt or Replace Mechanism

# Input Contract

- Read the selected source passages and mechanism derivations, including the notation correspondence, parameter choices, intermediate derivations, hypotheses at each use, and origins of constants.
- Record each source statement, hypothesis, scope, notation map, and authority tier exactly.
- Record its ambient spaces, norm or locally convex topology, dual pairing, and meanings of closure and continuity.
- Read the active target contract, condition profile, oracle model, and resource restrictions.
- Read verified route failures and source-applicability blockers when present.
- Treat orientation-only material as a mechanism lead, not as a citable theorem.
- Do not infer authority from a title, author, theorem label, or familiar-looking formula.
- Do not add a source hypothesis to the target merely because the source needs it.

# Procedure

1. State the precise target relation that the mechanism is intended to supply.
2. Extract the source theorem or derivation blueprint in its original quantified scope.
3. Match source objects to target objects with an explicit notation correspondence.
4. Carry out the supplied symbol and parameter substitutions through every selected intermediate relation rather than transferring only the source conclusion.
5. Compare every source hypothesis and condition at use with the active authorized premises.
6. Compare dimension, regularity, locality, topology, dual pairing, randomness, oracle, and method-class assumptions.
7. For separation, duality, or conjugacy mechanisms, verify convexity, closedness, qualification, continuity, and the exact product topology.
8. For closure, compactness, or net arguments, name each component topology and prove any uniform norm bound needed for the exact family; convergence of an arbitrary net alone does not supply it.
9. Prove that closure or relaxation preserves the required majorization in that topology; lower-semicontinuous convex closure does not automatically preserve a dual-pairing inequality.
10. For set-valued graph mechanisms, construct comparison points with simultaneous membership at one primal point; scaling, interpolation, or convexification needs its own graph-preservation proof.
11. Distinguish what the source proves: inequality, equality, exactness, attainment, representation, or maximality.
12. Identify boundary conditions, initialization, parameter ranges, and hidden domain restrictions.
13. Explain where each ordered intermediate relation or source-defined construction enters the target's dependency chain.
14. Preserve the stated constant provenance when specializing, combining, or solving the selected relations.
15. Prove any missing applicability bridge from active premises when possible.
   For local or regional mechanisms, make the applicability bridge explicit for every queried,
   extrapolated, proximal, virtual, rejected, and connecting-segment point. A source theorem about
   a fixed region or accepted sequence cannot be transferred by merely declaring that the target
   trajectory is in that region; the transfer must supply an independent containment argument.
16. If direct citation is unauthorized, rederive the needed relation from the exposed mechanism.
17. Treat labels such as standard or classical as leads, never substitutes for source authority.
18. Preserve constants and scope during specialization; do not silently strengthen assumptions.
19. Check that notation substitution does not change a supremum, expectation, or quantifier order.
20. Check that the adapted conclusion is strong enough for its downstream target clause.
21. If adaptation fails, isolate the first exact hypothesis or relation that does not transfer.
22. Propose a replacement whose substantive mathematical ingredient differs at that obstacle; distinguish a concrete local adaptation from an ingredient still needing deeper research.
23. A failure of convex separation, one recovery map, or one localization lemma excludes only that ingredient unless an exhaustive impossibility proof is supplied.
24. Trace the affected downstream dependencies rather than attaching a new name to the old failed route; canonical route changes belong to Planner.
25. For composite proximal routes, test centering, shifted-prox cancellation, and coupled Lyapunov structure.
26. If a moving-center comparison has no valid transfer lemma, replace it with a route whose analysis stays in one authorized parameterized family, such as a virtual-step continuation.
   Likewise, replace any adaptive-curvature argument whose rejection/high-level accounting has no
   proved monotone potential or disjoint charging map. A familiar dyadic line-search pattern is a
   search heuristic, not a transferable rate lemma.
27. For a research claim, state the obstacle or alternative as a precise proposed target without silently replacing the assigned question.
28. Keep unverified transfer ideas at hypothesis or search-only evidence level.

# Role-Specific Use

- Planner can propose candidate mechanisms and their route implications. State
  what supports a candidate and which substantive ingredient is still unknown;
  naming a mechanism does not establish its readiness or correctness.
- Research can develop a fresh local adaptation or representation
  within its bounded assignment. Lightweight work still requires complete
  justification for every claimed result. When the obstacle calls for deeper
  conjecture or mechanism discovery, return the actual attempts, failed relation
  and needed research to Director with a reasoned SMI recommendation.
- Prover uses adaptations to assemble the complete candidate, retaining
  unresolved mechanism obligations as gaps rather than assumptions.

# Handoff and Failure Discipline

- A change of mathematical method can stay within the assignment. A broader
  mechanism search or route change is a proposed handoff to Director, not an
  instruction to expand a local attempt into an unbounded research programme.
- Hand off an exact local applicability bridge to direct gap closure when it is now well posed.
- Hand off a missing function, witness, sequence, or hard instance to object construction.
- Hand off a missing method with a success contract to algorithm construction.
- Hand off a fragile transfer statement to boundary or counterexample exploration.
- If a source requires an unavailable assumption, prove it, replace the mechanism, or expose it.
- Keep a rederived bridge visibly separate from the source statement that suggested it.
- Do not report source mismatch as refutation of the target theorem.
- Do not reuse a refuted ansatz without a new equation, representation, or structural ingredient.
- Record the precise obstacle so later work does not repeat the same failed transfer.
- A successful adaptation still becomes mathematical authority only inside a verified proof.
