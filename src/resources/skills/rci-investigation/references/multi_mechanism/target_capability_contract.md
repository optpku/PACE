# Candidate Frontier Protocol

Freeze the candidate's requirements before selecting mechanisms. Derive its
claim shape from the reviewed seed, frozen anchor, and exact work-item scope,
not from mechanisms that are convenient to propose.

The candidate states its mathematical objects, definitions, domains,
quantifiers, scope, legal assumptions, positive target, refutation target, and
validation checks. Positive and refutation targets must use the same scope and
assumptions.

A legal assumption is data or a premise copied from the frozen anchor. The
following are never legal assumptions:

- the desired theorem or either prove-or-refute outcome;
- a certificate, invariant, comparison object, or transfer still to construct;
- an unknown trajectory, optimizer, witness, bound, or solution;
- a mechanism output relabeled as an assumption.

Each candidate must remain a bounded, checkable, nonroutine mathematical task,
not a topic or bookkeeping task. Split independent valuable targets rather than
combining them into one ambiguous task.
