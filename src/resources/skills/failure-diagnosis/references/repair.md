# From a blocker to a changed argument

| Observation | What it establishes | Useful next work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A transition has no derivation. | A proof gap, not necessarily a false claim. | State and derive the missing implication. |
| An admissible object violates an intermediate lemma. | That lemma fails at its tested scope. | Find a weaker sufficient bridge or replace the route. |
| A source needs an absent hypothesis. | The citation does not yet apply. | Prove the hypothesis or replace that source ingredient. |
| A numerical test violates the formula. | A lead requiring exact or certified checking. | Recover an admissible symbolic witness. |
| A proposed witness violates a target premise. | It is not yet a target counterexample. | Repair the witness or report only its diagnostic scope. |
| A complete witness satisfies the target negation. | A candidate negative proof. | Assemble and submit it through ordinary verification. |

Do not treat a review label as a mathematical explanation. Open the candidate
and inspect the actual equation, hypothesis, or missing case. If a reviewer
misread a definition, make the relevant derivation explicit in a complete
revision; do not rely on arguing about the label.

Test the consumer's needs before replacing an entire route. A proof seeking
\(A\le B\) may only need \(A\le 2B\); a uniform estimate may be stronger
than the finite-horizon goal. A weaker bridge is useful only if the final
target still follows with the correct constants and conditions.

Document the attempted repair and its first new obstruction. Repeating the
same formula with a new name is not a different mathematical ingredient.
Where discovery itself is the hard work, explain the open mechanism question
to Director instead of presenting a speculative step as ready for proof.
