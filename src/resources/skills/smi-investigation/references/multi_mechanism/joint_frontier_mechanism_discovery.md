# Joint Frontier-Mechanism Discovery

Use serious calculation and counterexample pressure to develop the question
and its mechanism. An open direction may need a precise conjecture; an assigned
exact problem already supplies the question and may instead need its central
move discovered. A supplied statement is a legitimate starting point even when
its mechanism is not understood.

## Competing Hypotheses

Before extending a chain, compare at least two plausible mechanism hypotheses
when the available mathematics permits it. Distinguish them by proof object,
key move, first nonroutine step, likely obstruction, and the concrete
calculation that would discriminate between them. Prefer a mechanism because it
explains the observed structure and survives the named attack, not because its
vocabulary resembles the target.

Do not add a mechanism merely to lengthen the route. Add one only when it
derives a genuinely missing intermediate fact, connects two previously
separate arguments, or exposes a new falsifiable obstruction. An accepted
predecessor is a candidate dependency, not a theorem that may be silently
assumed.

## Concrete Mathematical Work

Every proposed move must identify an actual proof object and show the first
nonroutine calculation in enough detail to check signs, constants, domains,
boundary cases, and quantifiers. Record derived steps in logical order and mark
conjectural steps honestly. Run a positive toy calculation and a hostile
in-scope test that could invalidate the move. Compare the nonroutine residue
with the closest cited result after subtracting standard consequences.

## Frontier Revision

For an open direction, let calculations refine the candidate when they reveal
a false statement, a missing authorized assumption, an ambiguous quantifier,
an incorrect positive/refutation duality, a routine consequence or an unbounded
research program. State the exact semantic change and why the evidence requires it.

For an assigned exact problem, revise the mechanism or report the concrete
counterexample or unresolved bridge. Do not replace that problem to evade the
obstacle or manufacture novelty. Any proposed change of statement must be an
explicit in-scope proposal in the existing route-integration handoff.

Do not weaken the frontier merely to make the current mechanism pass. A valid
revision must retain a nontrivial mathematical question, preserve the target
anchor boundary, and explain why proving, refuting, or partially advancing the
new statement would matter. If no such revision exists, reject the route.

## Emission Standard

Emit only when the frontier is explicit enough to hand directly to a prover,
the selected mechanisms give correct substantive support, the strongest
concrete counterexample attack has failed, and one to three finite genuinely
mathematical cores remain. Independent cores split; coupled cores must explain
their coupling. A mechanism route is evidence that the conjecture is grounded;
it is not evidence that the frontier is true or fully covered.
